# Every finite blindness pattern can be realized by Boolean observations

**A Lean-checked realization lemma and a warning about unrestricted topological models of information**

Date: 2026-08-30  
Artifact status: Lean-checked locally and frozen in Git commit `7910bd4508e384debab8b4d82e2cffad166616a6`  
Historical-novelty status: **not established and not claimed**

## Plain-language summary

Imagine a collection of yes/no sensors and a hidden yes/no fact. A group of
sensors is **blind** when two possible worlds can give that group exactly the
same readings even though the hidden fact differs between the worlds.

Blindness is automatically downward closed: if a group cannot determine the
fact, then throwing away some of its sensors cannot help. Consequently, the
blind groups form an abstract simplicial complex.

The realization theorem proves the converse. Given *any* desired finite
simplicial complex, one can construct a finite collection of possible worlds,
a Boolean target, and Boolean sensors whose blind groups are exactly the faces
of that complex.

This is primarily a **no-go result**. Without restrictions on the possible
worlds or sensors, the topology of blindness has no special universal shape.
Every finite pattern of holes and homology can occur. A theory that wants
topology to reveal a genuine information law must first impose meaningful
structure—probabilistic, algebraic, geometric, causal, computational, or
otherwise—on its observations.

## Exact statement

Let:

- `I` be a set of observation coordinates;
- `X` be a set of possible states;
- `q : X -> Bool` be a Boolean target; and
- `r_i : X -> Bool` be the observation at coordinate `i`.

A coalition `S ⊆ I` is target-blind when there are states `x,y ∈ X` such that

1. `q(x) != q(y)`, and
2. `r_i(x) = r_i(y)` for every `i ∈ S`.

Now choose an arbitrary family of Boolean generators

`F_j : I -> Bool`, indexed by `j ∈ J`.

There is an explicit state system for which

`S is blind  <->  there exists j such that S ⊆ F_j`.

For a finite simplicial complex `K`, take the `F_j` to be its maximal faces.
The blind coalitions are then exactly the faces of `K`.

The Lean theorem is slightly more general than the finite-complex corollary:

```lean
theorem blind_iff_below_generator {I J : Type} (F : J -> I -> Bool)
    (S : I -> Prop) :
    Blind S (State.observe F) State.target <->
      exists j, forall i, S i -> F j i = true
```

Stable theorem identifiers:

- `NewMathDiscovery.BlindnessRealization.blind_iff_below_generator`
- `NewMathDiscovery.BlindnessRealization.blind_downward`

## Construction and proof idea

Create one base state `z` and one face state `y_j` for each generator `F_j`.

- The target is false at `z` and true at every `y_j`.
- Every sensor reports false at `z`.
- At `y_j`, sensor `i` reports false exactly when `i` belongs to `F_j`, and
  reports true otherwise.

Thus `z` and `y_j` agree at coordinate `i` exactly when `i ∈ F_j`. They agree
on every coordinate in `S` exactly when `S ⊆ F_j`. Since the only
opposite-target pairs are a base state and a face state, these are all the
blind coalitions.

## Three-sensor example

Suppose the desired maximal blind groups are `{A,B}` and `{B,C}`. Use this
table:

| State | Target | A | B | C |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `z` | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `y_AB` | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| `y_BC` | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 |

The group `{A,B}` is blind because `z` and `y_AB` both show `(0,0)` there but
have different targets. Likewise `{B,C}` is blind using `y_BC`. The group
`{A,C}` is not blind: every target-true state differs from `z` on at least one
of those two sensors.

The complete blind family is

`empty, {A}, {B}, {C}, {A,B}, {B,C}`,

which is precisely the simplicial complex generated by the two chosen maximal
faces.

## Why this is useful

1. **It prevents an invalid inference.** Interesting homology in a blindness
   complex cannot, by itself, certify a universal privacy or information law.
   Arbitrary topology is realizable.
2. **It identifies the real research question.** Instead of classifying
   unrestricted blindness complexes, classify those realizable under a
   justified restricted observation language—for example linear sensors,
   bounded-noise channels, causal measurements, geometric range queries, or
   low-complexity circuits.
3. **It generates adversarial benchmarks.** Choosing maximal faces gives a
   Boolean data table with any desired blindness topology. Such tables can
   test privacy, feature-sufficiency, explanation, and inference algorithms.
4. **It provides a compact formal baseline.** The construction needs one base
   state plus one state per generator and is checked by Lean without
   problem-specific axioms.

## What it does not prove

- It is not cryptographic secrecy, differential privacy, causal
  non-identifiability, or a probabilistic statement.
- It uses existential blindness: one indistinguishable opposite-target pair
  is enough.
- It does not say topology is useless after natural structural restrictions
  are imposed.
- It does not establish that the realization lemma is historically new.
- The general generator theorem is formalized; the finite
  maximal-face/simplicial-complex wrapper is presently a short paper
  corollary rather than a separate Lean declaration.

## Prior-art boundary and novelty assessment

The construction sits at the intersection of several established languages:

- In database theory, `S` is non-blind exactly when the columns in `S`
  functionally determine the target column. Armstrong's dependency theory and
  Armstrong relations are therefore close antecedents: W. W. Armstrong,
  [Dependency Structures of Data Base Relationships](https://apollo.inf.upol.cz/~urbanec/teaching/2021/data2/files/armstrong.pdf)
  (1974), and Beeri–Dowd–Fagin–Statman,
  [On the Structure of Armstrong Relations for Functional Dependencies](https://doi.org/10.1145/2422.322414)
  (1984).
- The generator incidence relation has the blindness complex as its Dowker
  complex. This is the standard relation-to-complex construction; see Michael
  Robinson,
  [Cosheaf Representations of Relations and Dowker Complexes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12348).
- Losing coalitions in a simple game are exactly a simplicial complex, and the
  correspondence runs both ways; see Leah Valentiner and Ismar Volić,
  [The topology of simple games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12578).
- Dowker complexes have already been used to study inference and privacy; see
  Michael Erdmann,
  [Topology of Privacy](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.04130).

The responsible conclusion is therefore:

- **Useful:** yes, as a clean no-go lemma, a Boolean witness generator, and a
  Lean-checked falsifier for overstrong topological claims.
- **Distinct:** the exact target-blindness formulation and Lean artifact are a
  specific, reusable packaging of the idea.
- **Historically novel mathematics:** not defensible from the present search.
  The combinatorial core is very close to, and arguably an immediate instance
  of, established Dowker, simple-game, and functional-dependency machinery.
- **Potentially new research:** the restricted realization problem may be
  substantive, but no theorem about a restricted class is claimed here.
